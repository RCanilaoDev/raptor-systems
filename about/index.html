HQCP // Human Quality Control Protocol

Detection is not diagnosis.

Tools are good at finding signals. They are not automatically good at deciding what those signals mean for a specific website.

Raptor uses a documented verification ladder for material findings. A crawler, AI system, analytics platform, validator, or SEO suite can contribute evidence. Human verification determines whether the finding is trustworthy enough to become a recommendation.

Method // Decision Control

Verification, priority, and implementation order are different decisions.

A problem can be real and still not be the next thing worth fixing. Raptor separates those decisions so effort goes toward the work with the strongest combination of impact, confidence, and practical execution value.

Raptor // Working Principles

Technical depth underneath. Plain English on top.

The client should understand what is wrong, why it matters, what the evidence says, and what happens next without needing to become an SEO engineer or web developer.

01Evidence before recommendation.

A tool alert is the beginning of the investigation, not the end.

02Platform-agnostic thinking.

The method should survive a change in CMS, analytics platform, crawler, or SEO suite.

03No tool score presented as strategy.

Scores can help locate problems. Business context determines whether those problems matter.

04Implementation is part of the diagnosis.

Dependencies, production risk, and validation matter when deciding what should happen next.

05Clear client communication.

Technical findings are translated into decisions, not hidden behind jargon.

06Human control stays in the loop.

Automation and AI can accelerate evidence gathering. They do not replace final judgment.
