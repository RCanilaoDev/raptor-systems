Selected Operating Experience // Search Systems

Complex Local-Service Search Architecture

A combined property-management and cleaning business needed its unusual service model translated into a structure customers and search systems could understand.

Disclosure

This work predates Raptor Consulting Group and is presented as prior professional operating experience. Client identity is withheld.

CASE // 04Search Systems
SITUATIONCombined local services → clear search architecture
PROOF TYPESystem Delivery + Operating Method
STATUSANONYMIZED
CASE RULENo claim beyond the evidence.
01 // Situation

The operating problem.

The business combined property-management and cleaning services in a way that generic service templates did not explain accurately. The website needed to preserve the relationship between those offers without making the company appear fragmented or unclear.

The architecture also had to reflect how prospective customers described the problem, how local service intent differed by page, and which business details could be supported visibly and through structured data.

02 // Work Performed

What the operator actually did.

The case is described through discovery, architecture, and implementation planning rather than unsupported performance claims.

01Business Discovery

Document the operating model, service boundaries, customer types, geographic context, and relationship between property management and cleaning.

02Customer Language

Translate how customers described their needs into clearer topics, page intent, labels, and navigation paths.

03Service Architecture

Define parent, child, and related-service relationships so the website explained the combined model without collapsing distinct search intent.

04Search Signals

Plan content structure, heading hierarchy, metadata, internal links, and supported structured-data relationships around the visible business model.

03 // Result

What changed.

SYSTEM DELIVERED

The combined service model was translated into a clearer website and search architecture.

The case supports an architecture and operating-method claim. It does not claim a ranking increase, traffic lift, lead increase, or revenue outcome that was not measured and retained.

04 // Evidence

What supports the result statement.

The evidence identifies what was delivered and keeps the boundary between implementation and unverified business outcomes visible.

01
System DeliveryService relationships, page roles, navigation paths, and search architecture were defined around the actual operating model.
02
Operating MethodBusiness discovery and customer language informed the structure before metadata or schema planning.
03
Evidence BoundaryNo numerical search or business outcome is presented because a verified measurement record was not retained.
05 // Raptor Relevance

Why this experience matters to the current system.

This work reinforces a central Raptor principle: website architecture should begin with the business, the customer, and the relationships between services. Search signals should describe that verified structure, not substitute for it.
