Selected Operating Experience // Web Operations

High-Volume Website Production

A production environment moving roughly 30 websites per week during urgency periods required intake control, two quality checks, client review, launch coordination, and escalation handling.

Disclosure

This work predates Raptor Consulting Group and is presented as prior professional operating experience. The volume describes the broader production environment, not a current Raptor delivery promise.

CASE // 05Web Operations
SITUATIONHigh volume → controlled production states
PROOF TYPEPrior Operating Experience
STATUSANONYMIZED
CASE RULEVolume requires control, not shortcuts.
01 // Situation

The operating problem.

Website production involved many projects moving through intake, client communication, build resources, domains, quality control, approval, launch, and monitoring at the same time.

Delays could originate from incomplete client information, builder availability, infrastructure problems, special technical requirements, or launch dependencies. Higher-touch ecommerce and special projects required direct handling while the broader queue continued moving.

02 // Work Performed

What the operator actually did.

The work combined production coordination, direct project ownership, quality control, escalation, and launch verification.

01Intake and Priority

Review project information, identify missing requirements, distinguish standard work from special handling, and determine what could move next.

02Production Coordination

Guide projects through assigned build resources while tracking client notes, technical instructions, dependencies, and current production state.

03Two-Round Quality Control

Inspect the website through two quality checks before client review, then coordinate revisions without losing the approved scope or launch state.

04Launch and Monitoring

Coordinate domain and DNS work, confirm launch, connect required measurement systems, monitor the result, and escalate failures or special conditions.

03 // Result

What the operating system supported.

OPERATING VOLUME

The broader production environment could move roughly 30 websites per week during urgency periods while preserving staged review and client approval.

This is a prior operating-volume statement, not a claim that one person independently built every site or that Raptor currently promises the same weekly throughput.

04 // Evidence

What supports the result statement.

The evidence separates direct operating responsibility from the throughput of the wider production team.

01
Direct Operating ResponsibilityHigher-touch ecommerce, special projects, production escalation, quality control, and launch coordination were handled directly.
02
Defined WorkflowProjects moved through intake, production, two quality checks, client review, launch, and post-launch monitoring.
03
Volume BoundaryRoughly 30 websites per week describes urgency periods in the broader production environment, not individual build output.
05 // Raptor Relevance

Why this experience matters to the current system.

This environment shaped Raptor's current production principles: keep the work state visible, define ownership, separate standard and special handling, require quality checks, document dependencies, and treat post-launch verification as part of delivery.
